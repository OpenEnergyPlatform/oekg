    Workflow — from published heat plans to queryable data#

    This page describes the municipal heat planning pipeline: how a PDF published by a German municipality becomes structured, queryable data. It spans four repositories.

    For the OEKG's own, quite different workflow — authored on the platform rather than extracted from documents — see how the OEKG is populated.

    The pipeline#

    flowchart TD
    PDF["Published Warmeplan PDFs<br/>from German municipalities"]
    RAG["municipal-heat-planning-pdf-processing<br/>RAG pipeline<br/><b>now:</b> vocabulary identification<br/><b>planned:</b> attribute value extraction"]
    OUT["Extracted vocabulary<br/>and content"]
    MHPO["municipal-heat-planning-ontology<br/>MHPO"]
    OEO["Open Energy Ontology<br/>OEO"]
    KG["mhpkg<br/>this repository"]
    STORE[("Jena Fuseki dataset<br/>which one: undecided")]
    TAB[("Tables on the Open Energy Platform<br/>boundary: undecided")]
    USERS["Energy researchers<br/>modelling and planning"]

    PDF --> RAG
    RAG --> OUT
    OEO -- "imported from<br/>and aligned to" --> MHPO
    OUT --> MHPO
    MHPO --> KG
    OUT -. "bulk tabular data?" .-> TAB
    KG --> STORE
    STORE --> USERS
    TAB --> USERS

    Reading the diagram#

    Two boxes say undecided, and one arrow is a question mark. Those are real open questions, not gaps in the drawing. A diagram that quietly invented answers would be worse than one that admits them.

    The RAG pipeline does not yet extract values#

    Today the pipeline in municipal-heat-planning-pdf-processing runs with the goal of identifying the vocabulary used across published heat plans — what concepts appear, and how they are named. Extracting actual values for specific attributes is the intended next step, not a current capability.

    This distinction matters more than it might look. The research interest in this work is overwhelmingly in the values — real figures per municipality that planning and modelling work can build on. Anyone reading this diagram should not assume that data exists yet.

    The ontology is developed separately, and draws on OEO#

    MHPO is its own project. It both imports from and aligns to the Open Energy Ontology, which is where a good deal of the necessary energy-domain terminology already exists. This repository uses MHPO; it does not define it.

    Not everything belongs in a graph#

    Some of what the heat plans contain is naturally tabular — long series of figures per municipality, per year, per technology. Triples are a poor fit for that, and the Open Energy Platform already serves tables well.

    Where that boundary falls is not yet decided. Until it is, bulk tabular extractions are flagged rather than committed to the graph, so that the decision gets made deliberately instead of by accident. See Tech stack.

    Which Fuseki dataset#

    The Open Energy Platform already runs a Jena Fuseki store serving the OEKG. Whether the heat-planning graph becomes a second dataset in that same store, or gets its own instance, is an open hosting question with access, backup and governance attached to it.

    What this repository contributes#

    Only the box labelled mhpkg. The pipeline's other stages live in their own repositories, and this repository deliberately does not vendor them:

    Stage Where it lives
    PDF processing, RAG municipal-heat-planning-pdf-processing
    The ontology municipal-heat-planning-ontology
    Energy-domain terminology Open Energy Ontology
    The graph this repository, mhpkg/
    Serving and consumption Jena Fuseki, the Open Energy Platform

    The source PDFs are not vendored either — they are published documents and are referenced, not copied.
